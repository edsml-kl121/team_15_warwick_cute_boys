# Zippy web frontend

Run `docker compose up --build frontend` from the repository root, then open [localhost:3000](http://localhost:3000). For a frontend-only preview, run `python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory FE` and open [localhost:8765](http://localhost:8765).

The keyboard replaces the three dock controls with a message bar and **Back**. **Talk to Zippy** opens a live voice conversation over `WS /api/voice` (`realtime.js`). Tap it once and the microphone stays open: Zippy hears you through OpenAI Realtime, answers in its own voice as soon as you stop, and you can talk over it to interrupt. Your words and Zippy's appear in the chat, and cards and quick replies follow each spoken reply. Tap again, ✕ or switch to the keyboard to end the conversation. Use headphones if Zippy keeps interrupting itself, because its voice is reaching the microphone. Voice audio is billed per minute, so end the conversation when you're done. The two AudioWorklets (`audio-recorder.worklet.js`, `audio-playback.worklet.js`) move 24 kHz 16-bit PCM in and out.

If live voice can't start, the microphone falls back to browser dictation: it displays detected words below the three buttons and sends the message about 0.3 seconds after the browser marks a finished sentence (Chrome does), or after 2.5 seconds of silence in browsers that don't. Tap the middle waveform to send sooner. Zippy then reads its reply aloud with the browser's own voice and opens the microphone again. Tap the waveform while Zippy speaks to interrupt it. Silence, ✕ or switching to the keyboard ends the conversation. Typed messages stay silent. If the browser ends dictation on its own, as Safari on iPhone does after a short silence, it starts listening again and keeps the words so far. Browser dictation needs microphone permission and a supported speech recognition engine, such as Google Chrome's. While Zippy works, a short fixed line beside the typing dots says what it is doing, chosen from the backend's `tool` events. Reply text streams first. Cards and quick replies appear after it. On narrow screens, open **Chat history** for the left drawer. On wider screens, history appears beside the chat. History is stored for the current browser tab.

Tap the top-right **Profile** icon to open your profile. **New chat** is in the **Chat history** drawer, alongside **Today**, **Food log**, **Profile** and **Connect apps**. These pages read the backend's `/today`, `/food-log` and `/profile` routes. The drawer's **Presenter** section triggers demo moments and opens `presenter.html`, a dashboard for every trigger flow. To install on iPhone, open the HTTPS site in Safari, tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**. Web push setup and the presenter key are described in [CHAT_API.md](CHAT_API.md#web-push).

Use the camera button in chat or on the Food log page to upload a meal photo (JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 10 MB). When the backend has an OpenAI key, the scan shows upload, analysis and saving progress, then displays the estimated calories, macros and portions. **View Food log** opens the saved day.

Read [CHAT_API.md](CHAT_API.md) for the FastAPI connection, checkout, optional transcription, and original demo values. The static preview opens with an empty conversation. Sending requires the API.

In **Chat history**, select the trash icon next to a conversation and confirm to delete it. Deleting the current conversation opens a blank chat.

Open `/presenter.html` or `/presentor.html` on the Caddy-served app. Enter the phone's guest display name and the presenter key, then click **Trigger** beside **Recommend food** or **Recommend an item**. The recommendation appears in the guest’s chat.

## Verification

From the repository root, run `node --test FE/tests/*.cjs`, `node --check` on `FE/app.js`, `FE/markdown.js`, `FE/pages.js`, `FE/push.js`, `FE/presenter.js`, `FE/sw.js`, `FE/voice.js`, `FE/realtime.js` and `FE/services.js`, and `docker compose config --quiet`. In the browser, open and close the keyboard and history drawer, open each page from the menu, trigger each presenter flow, then verify message sending and how the app handles missing data. On a microphone-enabled browser, tap the microphone and ask about last night's sleep: your words, Zippy's spoken reply and a sleep card should appear, the microphone should stay open for a follow-up, talking over Zippy should stop its voice, and tapping the microphone again should end the conversation.
